    Freshwater Tanks

    Your freshwater tanks are the heart of your domestic water supply, storing clean water for every onboard system that depends on it. Understanding the purpose of each pipe on the tank lid, how the tank breathes, how the sender functions, and how to safely open and maintain the tank is essential knowledge for reliable cruising.

    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3dAVi3ZYlFc

    This guide walks you through the tank layout, basic operation, common troubleshooting points, and recommended cleaning procedures.

    Location & Access

    On most Balance Catamarans, the freshwater tanks are located midships in each hull.\nAccess is gained through the bilge, where you'll find the tank lid (flange) with all fittings clearly visible.

    Components on the Tank Lid (What Each Pipe Does)

    The tank lid is home to several critical fittings. Understanding each one helps with diagnostics and maintenance.

    Deck Fill Pipe (Water Fill)

    • Located on deck, this is where you fill the tank.
    • Water enters the tank through the large fill pipe attached to the flange.

    Freshwater Pick-Up (Supply to Pump)

    • This line feeds water from the tank to your freshwater pump system.
    • The pick-up runs directly to the bilge-mounted strainer and pump.

    Tank Breather (Vent Line)

    • Allows air to enter while drawing water, and exit when filling.
    • Without this, the tank would vacuum, collapse, or overflow.

    Watermaker Product Line

    • This small-diameter pipe feeds watermaker-produced freshwater back into the tank.
    • Only used when topping up via your onboard desalination system.

    Tank Sender (Water Level Sensor)

    • Reports your tank's water level to the digital gauges at your electrical/control panel.
    • If you see inaccurate readings or no percentage displayed, the sender is the likely culprit.
    • The sender is interchangeable—owners typically keep a spare.

    Replacing a Faulty Tank Sender

    A sender is easy to replace:

    1. Remove the bolts holding the sender to the flange.
    2. Gently pull the sender out of the tank.
    3. Disconnect the wiring plug.
    4. Connect the new sender.
    5. Insert it back into the tank and re-bolt evenly.

    This is a simple 5–10 minute task and a good spare to keep onboard.

    Keeping Your Freshwater Tanks Clean

    Sediment and debris in the tank often reveal themselves when you find dirty strainers in your freshwater pump system.

    There are two main cleaning methods:

    Method A: Deep Clean Via Tank Flange

    Ideal when you want to physically inspect and wipe down the interior.

    Steps:

    1. Open the tank lid (see flange-opening procedure below).
    2. Shine a torch inside for visual inspection.
    3. Pump or scoop out remaining water.
    4. Wipe down walls with:
    5. Microfibre cloth
    6. Food-safe sterilising solution (Milton tablets or Milton liquid)

    Method B: Chemical Clean Without Opening

    A simpler routine clean:

    • Drop Milton sterilising tablets or liquid into the tank.
    • Fill the tank and let the solution circulate.
    • Flush through outlets after the dwell period.

    Frequency:\nEvery 6 months is ideal, though some owners go 12 months if they only use high-quality dockside water.

    How to Safely Open the Tank Flange (Important!)

    Warning

    Improper flange handling can cause leaks or damage. Use a careful criss-cross approach.

    Opening the Flange

    • Loosen bolts in a cross pattern, not clockwise around the rim.
    • This prevents the flange plate from distorting.
    • If the large water fill pipe is still attached, you'll have limited lift, but often enough to peek inside with a torch.
    • For full removal, disconnect the fill hose and any attached lines.

    Critical Checks

    Before reassembly:

    • Ensure sealing surfaces are clean.
    • Inspect flange gasket / flange material—replace if deteriorated.
    • Confirm no debris sits between the flange and tank opening.

    Tightening the Flange

    • Lower the lid evenly.
    • Tighten bolts in a criss-cross (zigzag) pattern again.
    • Uneven tightening can pinch the gasket, deform the lid, and cause a leaking flange.

    Take your time: a perfect seal is essential to prevent contamination and odours.

    Best Practices for Long Tank Life

    • Keep tanks sealed and capped at all times.
    • Avoid filling with poor-quality dockside water whenever possible.
    • Inspect strainers often—dirty strainers usually mean tank contamination.
    • Sanitise tanks every 6–12 months.
    • Ensure the breather pipe is always clear and unobstructed.
    • Carry a spare tank sender for long passages.
    • Never apply sealants to a flange unless specified—use the gasket materials provided.

    Final Notes

    Your freshwater tanks form the foundation of your domestic plumbing. With proper cleaning, correct flange handling, and a clear understanding of each fitting, they will reliably support the freshwater demands of your Balance Catamaran for years to come.
