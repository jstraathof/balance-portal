    Freshwater Pumps

    Essential Knowledge

    Your freshwater pump system is responsible for delivering pressurised water from your tanks to every freshwater outlet on board—sinks, showers, basins, deck wash, cockpit shower, and even freshwater heads if fitted. Knowing how this system works, where its components are located, and how to recognise (and solve) early signs of trouble is an essential onboard skill for every Balance owner.

    https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JFGa7E50n60

    System Overview

    On Balance Catamarans, your freshwater pumps are typically located in:

    • Port mid-bilge
    • Starboard mid-bilge

    Each side houses a self-contained pump "cluster," which includes:

    • Inlet Supply Line – draws water from the tank
    • Strainer – catches debris before it reaches the pump
    • Freshwater Pump – creates the pressure to move water through the boat
    • Accumulator Tank – stabilises pressure for smooth flow and reduced cycling
    • Isolation Valve – allows you to shut off flow for service or troubleshooting
    • Electrical Junction Box – houses the wiring and pump connections

    You can easily identify the system by the combination of accumulator tank and the adjacent pump.

    Once activated from your electrical panel, the pump will only run when the system senses a pressure drop. The accumulator tank releases water first; as that pressure falls, the pump kicks in automatically to recharge it.

    How to Identify Normal vs Abnormal Operation

    Your freshwater pump has a very distinct sound profile—steady, rhythmic, and smooth.\nBecoming familiar with this sound is the #1 diagnostic tool you have.

    The pump may:

    • Stutter
    • Choke
    • Grind
    • Cycle intermittently
    • Run when no tap is open
    • Stay on permanently
    • Not switch on at all

    Any of these behaviours signal a fault that needs attention.

    Most Common Causes (in order of likelihood)

    1. Freshwater outlet slightly open or dripping
    2. Airlock in the system
    3. Freshwater tank empty
    4. Blockage (usually strainer)
    5. Leak in the plumbing or fittings
    6. Electrical supply issue
    7. Faulty or broken pump

    Troubleshooting Guide

    Intermittent Operation

    A dripping faucet or shower head will cause the accumulator to slowly empty, triggering the pump repeatedly.

    Solution:\nDo a quick walk-around and ensure all outlets are fully closed.

    Clearing Airlocks

    Airlocks are extremely common—often caused by running a tank dry.

    Steps to purge the system:

    1. Confirm the tank has water.
    2. Ensure the pump is switched on.
    3. Make sure the isolation valve is open (parallel to the pipe).
    4. Close the freshwater crossover (see our "Crossover" video for help).
    5. Open faucets on the same side as the pump experiencing the issue.

    You'll hear spitting as the air exits the lines.\nAfter a short while, flow should normalise and the pump should regain its normal rhythm.

    Check the Strainer

    A clogged strainer restricts water flow and starves the pump.

    How to inspect and clean it:

    • Look through the strainer's clear top for debris
    • Twist it open
    • Rinse under a tap
    • Reinstall

    Tip

    Make a habit of glancing at your strainers whenever you're working in the bilges.

    Electrical Checks

    A bad connection can cause intermittent or total loss of pump function.

    Steps:

    1. Switch off both pumps (in case the crossover is open).
    2. Open the junction box.
    3. Inspect all connections.
    4. Use a multimeter to confirm 12V supply is present.

    If power is present and wiring is solid, you may have a failed pump.

    Signs of a Failing or Broken Pump

    A failing pump may produce:

    • Grinding sounds
    • Rough pulsations
    • Slow cycling
    • No cycling at all

    If the pump is broken and you are underway without a spare:

    Use the freshwater crossover to allow the functioning pump to serve both hulls temporarily.

    How to Replace a Freshwater Pump

    If a pump needs replacement, follow this sequence:

    1. Turn off pump power
    2. Close the isolation valve
    3. Open the junction box
    4. Remove wiring from the Wago connectors
    5. Release the John Guest fittings (supply & outbound lines)
    6. Unscrew the four mounting points holding the pump platform
    7. Remove the entire assembly
    8. Replace the pump
    9. Reassemble in reverse order

    This is a straightforward maintenance task that most owners can handle with basic onboard tools.

    Best Practices for Long-Term Reliability

    • Never let freshwater tanks run completely dry
    • Inspect strainers regularly
    • Get familiar with your pump's normal sound and rhythm
    • Exercise all isolation valves occasionally
    • Keep a spare freshwater pump on board for long passages
    • Check for leaks anytime bilge areas are opened

    Final Notes

    A freshwater pump may be a small component—yet it is central to daily comfort on board. With a clear understanding of how the system works and early recognition of unusual pump behaviour, most issues can be resolved quickly and confidently.
